The ten plays

Each play names the grind, what runs instead, how you know it is working, and what the record shows the inspector.

■Play 1 · The policy question, answered with the page number

A carer asks what the policy says. The answer comes back from your own documents, with the page it came from, and nothing leaves the building.

■Play 2 · The inbox that sorts itself

Family emails, supplier chasers and agency confirmations are sorted and drafted for a person to send. A person still sends.

■Play 3 · The five o'clock report

What happened today, pulled together from the records you already keep, on the manager's desk before they leave.

■Play 4 · The training matrix that chases itself

Who is due, who is overdue, and the reminder already written. The matrix stops being a spreadsheet somebody updates on a Sunday.

■Play 5 · DBS and right to work that never lapse

Expiry dates watched every day, the renewal started in time, the evidence filed where the inspector will look.

■Play 6 · The incident write up

The facts in the right order and the right form, drafted from the notes, for the manager to check and sign.

■Play 7 · The complaint acknowledged on day one

The acknowledgement goes the same day, in your tone, and the timeline starts clean.

■Play 8 · Family updates, warm and on time

The weekly note to families drafted from the record, read and sent by a person who knows the resident.

■Play 9 · The applicant who hears back the same day

Every application answered, the strong ones flagged, the interview offered, before the applicant takes the other job.

■Play 10 · The audit pack before the inspector arrives

The evidence you already hold, gathered into the pack that supports Regulation 17 and 19, ready before the visit is announced.

Also inside

■The one rule

No resident, client or staff data goes into a public AI tool, and a person checks anything before it goes out. Everything else follows from that.

■Ten questions to ask any vendor, including us

Where does our data go, who decides, what is the record, and what happens when it is wrong. Keep the answers.

■Your first thirty days

Measure one thing for a fortnight, pick one play, run it for a fortnight, and compare. No supplier needed for any of it.
