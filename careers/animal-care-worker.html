Animal care worker

If animals settle when you are near, this branch is for you. Animal care workers keep kennels, catteries, stables and rescue centres running: feeding, grooming, exercising, cleaning and watching for the small signs that something is wrong. It is hands-on, sometimes messy, often outdoors in all weathers, and entry pay is modest. The reward is daily contact with animals and a clear growth route: many people move on to veterinary care assistant roles, and with training, veterinary nursing.

A day in this job

Mornings begin with feeds, fresh water and cleaning out, whatever the weather. You exercise the dogs, groom the horses, or sit quietly with a nervous rescue cat until it trusts you. Through the day you watch every animal for limps, off days and changes in appetite, and you note what you see. The last round of the evening makes sure everyone is settled, fed and safe.

How AI is changing it

AI now helps with bookings, feeding schedules, health records and reminders, and smart cameras can watch animals overnight. That takes away a lot of clipboard work. The calm hands, the gentle voice and the instinct that says this animal is not right today stay completely human.

What you need to start

You can start without formal qualifications, though some employers ask for GCSEs. Animal care qualifications and apprenticeships, including horse care, are there when you want to move up.

Typical pay: £450 a week (about £23,400 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Explore animal care roles Read profiles for kennel, cattery, stable and rescue centre work to see what each involves.
  2. Volunteer with animals Rescue centres and stables often welcome volunteers. It is the best way to test the work and be noticed.
  3. Under 30? Get extra support The King's Trust helps 16 to 30 year olds into work with courses, grants and confidence.
  4. Take an animal care qualification Animal care qualifications at levels 1 and 2 fit around work, and apprenticeships cover horse care too.
  5. Apply and start Kennels, catteries, stables, rescue centres and vet practices all take on care staff. Reliability counts most.
  6. Grow towards veterinary nursing Experienced care workers can move into veterinary care assistant roles, and with further training, veterinary nursing.
