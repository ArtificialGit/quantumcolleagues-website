Business-support / admin officer

Business-support officers are the engine room of a council: minutes, records, bookings, invoices and the small tasks that keep services running. It suits reliable people who like order and want a stable start in public service. Be honest about it: the pay is modest, some of the work is repetitive, and you are rarely the visible one. It is still one of the best doors in, because councils hire for these roles all the time and promote from them often.

A day in this job

Mornings usually mean working through the team inbox and updating records. You might minute a meeting, book rooms and travel, or process invoices before lunch. Afternoons bring more of the same plus queries from colleagues who need something found or fixed. You finish by clearing the day's list.

How AI is changing it

AI now drafts minutes, letters and standard replies, and keeps records tidy with less typing. That shifts the role rather than removing it. What stays yours is the human glue: knowing who to ask, spotting what looks wrong and being the person the team relies on.

What you need to start

There are no minimum entry requirements, though most people have GCSEs. Training happens on the job, with administration qualifications available at Levels 2 and 3.

Typical pay: £500 a week (about £26,000 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. See the route in Read business-support and admin profiles to see why this is the classic first job inside a council.
  2. Sharpen writing and numbers Clear writing, careful numbers and tidy digital admin are the daily currency of every council office.
  3. Watch the council jobs board Councils advertise business-support roles constantly. Set a weekly reminder and apply quickly when one fits.
  4. Consider a funded apprenticeship Many councils run business-administration apprenticeships open at any age. You earn while you train.
  5. Apply well Council applications ask you to meet each point in the person specification. Answer every one plainly.
  6. Grow once you're in Internal moves are strong. Learn the departments, take the training offered and aim for the team you want.
