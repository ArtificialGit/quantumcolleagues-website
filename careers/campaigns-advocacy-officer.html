Campaigns & advocacy officer

Campaigns and advocacy officers push for change: new policy, better services, fairer treatment for the people their charity serves. It suits persuasive, principled people who can write sharply, organise others and hold their nerve in public. The honest parts: change is slow, wins are shared, and campaign roles often ask for a degree and strong writing. It matters now because causes are crowded, attention is scarce, and organisations need people who can cut through honestly.

A day in this job

You might start by scanning the news for anything touching your cause. Mid-morning goes on drafting a briefing, a letter to an MP or campaign content. Afternoons could bring a coalition call, a supporter email and planning an action. Big moments, like a report launch, break the routine.

How AI is changing it

AI now drafts briefings, campaign copy and monitoring summaries, and tracks coverage overnight. Strategy and conviction do not come with it. What stays human is the campaigner's core: judging the moment, building alliances and speaking with real conviction for real people.

What you need to start

Most people come in with A levels and a degree or something similar. Further professional qualifications are available once you are in.

Typical pay: £770 a week (about £40,000 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Explore advocacy work Read campaigner and advocacy profiles to see how comms, organising and persuasion fit together in one job.
  2. Volunteer on a live campaign Join a charity or community campaign near you and learn the mechanics from the inside.
  3. Build your writing and comms Sharp writing and confident social posts are the campaigner's toolkit. Practise both, in public, often.
  4. Study the policy side for free Free courses on politics, policy and influencing deepen your case, useful because many roles expect a degree.
  5. Apply Charities, unions and campaign groups all hire officers. Lead with the campaign work you actually did.
  6. Grow once you're in Take the lead on one campaign strand, then build towards campaign manager or policy roles.
