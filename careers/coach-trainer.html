Coach / trainer

Years of hard-won experience are worth more than you probably think, and coaching or training is how you turn them into help other people pay for. This suits good listeners who genuinely enjoy watching someone else get better, and who can be honest kindly. The hard parts deserve honesty too: charging for advice feels strange at first, income builds slowly through referrals, and some clients will not do the work. What is new is that AI handles the bookings, notes, materials and follow-ups, so your energy goes into the sessions themselves rather than the admin around them.

A day in this job

Two coaching sessions today, one in the morning and one in the afternoon. Between them, AI has the session notes written up and the follow-up actions sent before you have made tea. You spend an hour improving one exercise in your programme. The diary, reminders and invoices look after themselves.

How AI is changing it

AI drafts your materials, manages the diary, writes up the notes and nudges clients between sessions. None of that is the job. What stays human is the listening, the judgement about what this person needs, the relationship, and the promise you make and keep.

What you need to start

There is no gate, so you can coach from day one. What matters is a clear audience, a repeatable programme, and a few honest testimonials earned before you charge.

The steps, in order

  1. Define who you help Choose one audience and one change you help them make, and say it in a sentence anyone would understand.
  2. Build a simple programme Design one repeatable session or short course, with a clear start, a clear finish and something people take away.
  3. Coach for free first Coach a few people at no charge to sharpen the programme and gather honest testimonials you can share.
  4. Sort the money basics Decide your price, set up simple invoicing and keep a tax pot, so charging feels normal from day one.
  5. Start charging Move early clients to paid, ask every happy one for a referral, and keep delivery quality boringly consistent.
  6. Grow the practice Add group sessions, workplace workshops or a waiting list once demand proves itself, without losing the personal touch.
