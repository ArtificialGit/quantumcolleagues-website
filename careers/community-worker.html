Community worker

Community workers run the clubs, sessions and support that hold a neighbourhood together, usually for a charity or a council. It suits people who listen well, organise without fuss and keep showing up. Be honest with yourself about the hard parts: pay is modest, funding comes in short cycles, and you will sometimes carry other people's problems home. It is worth considering now because charities are stretched, volunteering is a genuine front door, and the relationships you build become the job.

A day in this job

Mornings often start with a team catch-up and a look at who needs following up. You might run a youth session, visit a community centre or meet a family with a support worker. Afternoons are for phone calls, session notes and planning next week's activities. Most days end with something practical, like setting up a hall or locking one up.

How AI is changing it

AI now drafts session plans, case notes, funding updates and letters, which frees hours every week. What it cannot do is sit with someone, earn their confidence or speak up for them. The heart of this work stays human: listening, advocacy and knowing your patch and its people.

What you need to start

Most roles ask for a youth and community work qualification at Level 2 or 3, and apprenticeships are available. A DBS check is usually needed, and some employers ask you to be over 21.

Typical pay: £550 a week (about £28,600 a year).
Official UK pay for "Youth and community workers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Explore community jobs Read about community worker and project coordinator jobs to see what they involve.
  2. Take a regular volunteering slot Weekly beats now and then. It builds the references and relationships that get you hired.
  3. Under 30? Try the King's Trust Its community and into work programmes give you a strong start.
  4. Add safeguarding and admin basics A short safeguarding course is expected across charity work and easy to get.
  5. Run a small project Lead a stall, an event or an appeal. It proves you can deliver.
  6. Apply inside the network Most charity jobs go to people the organisation already knows. Now that's you.
