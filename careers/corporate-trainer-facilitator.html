Corporate trainer / facilitator

Trainers and facilitators stand up in front of teams and help them change: new systems, new skills and, lately, working well with AI. Every workplace adopting new tools needs someone who makes change feel doable rather than threatening. It suits warm, credible people who read a room and enjoy the energy of live sessions. The trade-offs are real: you are only as good as your last session, travel can pile up, and freelance bookings arrive unevenly. Demand is rising fast though, career changers are welcomed because lived experience is the credential, and firms pay properly for trainers who deliver.

A day in this job

The morning is a workshop with a team learning a new system, part teaching and part reassurance. You adjust the plan twice because the room needs something different from the script. The afternoon goes to feedback forms, a debrief call with the manager and tweaks to next week's session. You practise tomorrow's opening once before stopping.

How AI is changing it

AI drafts session plans, slides, handouts and follow-up notes, and can quiz learners between sessions, so preparation time is shrinking. What stays human is the room: reading faces, adjusting on the fly, giving people confidence, and being the person a team trusts to walk them through change.

What you need to start

No formal qualifications are needed to start; most trainers qualified in another field first, then added a teaching or training qualification, available at Levels 3 to 5. Professional qualifications from the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development also exist.

Typical pay: £670 a week (about £34,800 a year).
Official UK pay for "Other vocational and industrial trainers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Pick your topic Choose something you can genuinely teach. Helping teams work well with AI is in real demand right now.
  2. Design one session Build a short, practical workshop where people do things, not just watch slides. Practical beats polished.
  3. Practise for free Run sessions for community groups, charities or your own workplace. Confidence comes from repetitions, not theory.
  4. Add a funded qualification A teaching or training qualification adds weight, and funding is often available for courses at this level.
  5. Get hired or freelance Firms pay properly for trainers who make change feel doable. Offer a short taster session to open doors.
  6. Grow once you're in Turn one workshop into a programme. Trainers who measure what changed get invited back and paid more.
