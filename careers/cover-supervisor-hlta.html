Cover supervisor / HLTA

Cover supervisors and higher level teaching assistants step up to lead lessons: covering classes, running interventions, sometimes managing other assistants. It is the natural next rung for experienced teaching assistants who want more responsibility and better pay without full teacher training. Honestly, you carry classroom management pressure without a teacher's pay or planning time, and status varies between schools. But it proves you can hold a room, it pays noticeably more than standard TA work, and it is the strongest springboard into teaching.

A day in this job

You might start with a briefing on which classes need cover, then lead a lesson from the teacher's plan. Between covers you run booster groups or support other assistants. Behaviour is yours to hold, so your presence matters all day. After school there are notes to leave for the returning teachers.

How AI is changing it

Lesson resources, cover plans and marking are increasingly drafted or speeded up with AI, which wins your evenings back. Notes on how a class went can be dictated in minutes. Holding a room, earning respect and safeguarding judgement remain yours alone.

What you need to start

You need to achieve higher level teaching assistant status, and significant classroom experience is usually necessary. A DBS check is mandatory.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).
Official UK pay for "Higher level teaching assistants" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Build TA experience A year or two as a teaching assistant is the foundation, so gather range across different classes, ages and needs.
  2. Watch how teachers lead Ask to observe strong teachers managing behaviour, and note the routines and calm authority you want to make your own.
  3. Aim for HLTA status Higher level teaching assistant status is assessed against national standards, and schools often sponsor good staff through the process.
  4. Use AI to plan and mark Learn to draft resources, adapt worksheets and speed up marking with AI, always checking quality and safeguarding yourself.
  5. Apply for cover roles Cover supervisor posts appear every term, and your HLTA evidence plus real classroom stories make a strong application.
  6. Consider teaching later If leading lessons suits you, funded routes from TA into teaching are well supported, with bursaries for some subjects.
