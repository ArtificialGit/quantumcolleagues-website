Customer-service advisor

Council customer-service advisors are the first voice a resident hears, on the phone, online or at the front desk. It suits patient, unflappable people who can explain things clearly and stay kind under pressure. Honestly, it is demanding: some callers are upset or angry, targets are real, and pay is modest. It earns its place on this list because you learn how the whole council works within months, and advisors move into specialist teams all the time.

A day in this job

You log in and take your first calls or webchats within minutes. Each contact is different: a missed bin, a housing worry, a payment problem. Between calls you update records and pass complex cases to the right team. Breaks are scheduled, and the day has a clear rhythm.

How AI is changing it

AI now answers the simple, repeated questions and drafts responses for the rest. That leaves advisors the contacts that actually need a person. What stays human is the hard, important part: listening to someone upset, untangling a messy case and making a resident feel heard.

What you need to start

There are no formal entry requirements, though many employers expect GCSEs. Training is given on the job, topped up with short courses.

Typical pay: £530 a week (about £27,600 a year).
