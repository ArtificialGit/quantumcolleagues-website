Data analyst

Data analysts take the numbers an organisation collects and turn them into answers people can act on: which product sells, which service struggles, where the money goes. Most organisations gather far more data than they understand, so good analysts are wanted in every sector, from hospitals to football clubs. Patient, curious people who like asking why thrive here, and you do not need a computer science degree. It is screen heavy work and the tools keep changing, but the results are genuinely useful and the skills travel anywhere.

A day in this job

You spend the morning pulling data together and checking it is clean and complete. Then you dig into a question someone needs answered, testing ideas against the numbers. In the afternoon you might build a chart or a short report that makes the answer plain. You finish by talking the findings through with the people who will use them.

How AI is changing it

AI now writes many of the queries and drafts the charts, so analysts spend less time on routine number crunching. The job shifts towards asking the right question, checking the answer is sound, and taking responsibility for what it leads to. Knowing what the numbers mean for real people stays human.

What you need to start

Most entrants have a relevant degree, but vocational qualifications and apprenticeships are accepted routes in too.

Typical pay: £880 a week (about £45,800 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Start with free basics A free intro data course shows you whether you enjoy the work.
  2. Brush up your maths Being confident with numbers makes everything in data work easier.
  3. Learn one tool properly Start with spreadsheets, then try SQL or a dashboard tool. Free tutorials cover all of it.
  4. Do a funded data bootcamp Data Skills Bootcamps are built for people changing careers, and they are funded.
  5. Build two small projects Real work on real data counts for more than certificates alone.
  6. Get a qualification and apply A BCS data qualification gives your work extra weight with employers.
