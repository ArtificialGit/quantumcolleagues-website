Data governance officer

Data governance officers make sure an organisation uses information legally, fairly and carefully: who may see what, how long records are kept, whether the data behind decisions can be trusted. As rules around data and AI tighten, every sizeable organisation needs someone in this seat. It rewards people who are precise, principled and comfortable saying no, and many arrive from admin, legal or compliance backgrounds rather than from tech. The reading is heavy and the rules keep evolving, but the work protects real people.

A day in this job

Your morning might start with a team asking to use customer data in a new way; you check the rules and give a clear answer. Midday you review how one dataset is stored and who can reach it. The afternoon goes on rewriting a policy in plain English. You log every decision you made.

How AI is changing it

AI can summarise regulations, scan systems for risky data and draft policies in minutes. It cannot take accountability when a regulator calls, or weigh what is fair rather than merely allowed. Judgement, ethics and answerability stay human.

The steps, in order

  1. Learn what governance means Free courses cover data governance and standards: who may see what, how long records are kept, and why it matters.
  2. Get to know the rules UK data protection rules and data ethics are the core. Learn them well enough to explain them to a colleague.
  3. Practise where you are Offer to review how your team stores and shares information. Small, real improvements become your first governance evidence.
  4. Explore funded study routes Data apprenticeships and funded adult courses can formalise governance knowledge without you taking a career break.
  5. Add a credential A BCS data qualification underlines your seriousness in a field where employers value proof of rigour.
  6. Apply, then earn trust Governance roles are growing in every sector. Once in, grow by owning policies and becoming the person people ask first.
