E-learning developer

E-learning developers build the digital courses that learning designers plan: the interactive lessons, videos and quizzes that run inside company training systems. It sits between creative and technical work, and people who can genuinely build are scarcer than people who can plan. It suits tinkerers who like making things work and do not mind fiddly detail. It has dull stretches: some corporate content is dry, deadlines cluster, and the tools change often. The skills transfer everywhere though, remote work is common, and one polished module you built yourself is enough to start conversations with training teams.

A day in this job

You spend the morning building an interactive module: assembling screens, wiring up questions and testing every click. A designer sends revisions at lunch, so you rework a scenario and fix a stubborn animation. The afternoon goes to loading the course into the client's system and checking it works on phones. You back up and log the changes.

How AI is changing it

AI drafts course text, generates quiz questions, voices narration and helps debug builds, so raw production keeps getting faster. What stays human is the craft: making the experience feel smooth, checking the content is accurate and fair, and knowing what will actually help someone learn.

What you need to start

Entrants usually have a foundation course, a BTEC, a degree or similar behind them, and portfolio work is important for entry. Modules you have actually built are the strongest evidence you can show.

Typical pay: £700 a week (about £36,400 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Learn the tools Free courses cover authoring tools and how online courses fit together. You can practise on free trial versions.
  2. Build one module Turn something you know into a short interactive lesson. One finished module is your first real sample.
  3. Use AI to speed production AI drafts content, scenarios and question banks while you shape the learning and check it is fair.
  4. Check funded routes Skills Bootcamps cover digital skills free for many adults, and bigger qualifications often attract funding too.
  5. Show your builds Training teams hire people who can genuinely build. A working sample beats a certificate every time.
  6. Grow once you're in Learn what makes courses land, not just load. Developers who understand learners move up to design whole programmes.
