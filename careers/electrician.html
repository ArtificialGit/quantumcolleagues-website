Electrician

Electricians wire, test and repair the systems every building runs on. It suits people who like solving problems they can see and touch, who take pride in tidy, safe work, and who want a trade that travels anywhere. Be honest about the hard parts: crawling through lofts, cold sites, dusty floors, and kneeling more than your knees would like. The reason to consider it now is the electrification of everything. Heat pumps, EV chargers and solar all need qualified electricians, and there are not enough of them.

A day in this job

You start with a plan and a van full of kit. The morning might be first fix on a new build, threading cable through joists. The afternoon could be a fault-find in an older house, tracing a dead circuit with a tester. There is tea, dust, and the quiet satisfaction of the board going live cleanly.

How AI is changing it

AI now speeds up the paperwork side: quotes, certificates, scheduling and parts lookup can be drafted in minutes rather than evenings. Diagnostic tools are getting smarter at suggesting likely faults too. But the wiring, the judgement about what is safe in this particular house, and the trust a customer places in you remain entirely human.

What you need to start

You do not always need qualifications to start, though GCSEs help. Most people train through an apprenticeship or a college course, and you need good eyesight and normal colour vision.

Typical pay: £750 a week (about £39,000 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Explore electrical careers Read about electrician and heat pump engineer jobs to see routes and pay.
  2. Try it before you commit A taster day or evening course at a local college lets you test the trade.
  3. Under 30? Get a leg up The King's Trust runs programmes and grants that make the first steps easier.
  4. Start the training An electrical apprenticeship or college diploma is the recognised way in.
  5. Add the green specialisms Heat pump, EV charging and solar courses stack on top. Funded options exist.
  6. Certify and register Join the right competent person scheme and you can sign off your own work.
  7. Go out on your own (optional) Many electricians end up self-employed, picking their jobs and rates.
