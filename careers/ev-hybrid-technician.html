EV / hybrid technician

EV and hybrid technicians service and repair electric vehicles, working with high-voltage systems that most garages are still learning. It suits mechanics who enjoy the electrical side and want to be ahead of the change rather than chasing it. The honest bits: high-voltage work demands strict discipline, the training never really stops, and some days are software as much as spanners. Electric vehicles are a growing share of everything on the road, and technicians qualified to work on them safely are genuinely scarce.

A day in this job

A day might mix a battery health check, a charging fault and a routine service far quieter than any engine bay you trained on. You isolate the high-voltage system before touching anything, and prove it dead with your own meter. Software updates and diagnostics fill the gaps between the hands-on work.

How AI is changing it

The cars themselves report rich data, and AI turns it into likely causes before the bonnet opens. Scheduling, estimates and technical bulletins are quicker to handle too. What stays yours is the safe isolation, the physical repair and the calm judgement around systems that demand respect.

What you need to start

There are no formal entry requirements, although some employers ask for GCSEs. Training runs through an apprenticeship, typically taking three to four years.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).
