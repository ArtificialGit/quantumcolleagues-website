Family / community support worker

Family support workers help households through hard patches: routines, budgeting, school attendance, getting to appointments, and reaching the right services early, before crisis. It suits practical, non judgemental people who can hold a family's trust while gently being honest with them. The honest bit: caseloads can be heavy, and you will care about families whose problems you cannot fully solve. The work is deeply useful, councils and charities recruit steadily, and lived experience of hard times counts in your favour.

A day in this job

Mornings might be a home visit, helping a parent plan the week or prepare for a school meeting. You write up notes, chase referrals and phone other services between visits. Afternoons could bring a family group session or a difficult conversation handled kindly. No two days repeat.

How AI is changing it

Case notes, referral forms and reports are increasingly drafted with AI help, which frees hours for actual visits. Pattern spotting tools can flag families who need earlier help. Sitting at a kitchen table, listening without judgement and keeping trust stay human.

What you need to start

You need a qualification accredited by the National Youth Agency at Level 2 or 3, and apprenticeships are available. Some employers ask you to be over 21, and a DBS check is likely.

Typical pay: £550 a week (about £28,600 a year).
Official UK pay for "Youth and community workers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Understand the role Read family support and early help profiles to see the mix of practical help, listening and working with other services.
  2. Volunteer in the community Family centres, food banks and parenting charities all build relevant experience and show you the reality of the work.
  3. Add safeguarding basics Short free courses in safeguarding and early help are widely available, and employers notice candidates who arrive with them.
  4. Get the qualification Most roles ask for a National Youth Agency accredited qualification at Level 2 or 3, with apprenticeship routes available too.
  5. Apply to councils and charities Both recruit steadily, so apply across early help teams, family hubs and charities, and be honest about your experience.
  6. Grow once you're in Experienced workers progress into senior family work, social work training or specialist roles, often with funded part time study.
