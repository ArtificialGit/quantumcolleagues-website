Farm and livestock worker

Farming is real work with real rhythms: lambing in spring, harvest in late summer, early starts all year round. Modern farm work mixes animal husbandry with serious machinery, from GPS guided tractors to robotic milking parlours, so it rewards people who like both animals and kit. It is physical, and the hours flex with the seasons and the weather. Entry pay is modest, but there are no set qualifications to start, you learn by doing, and food never goes out of fashion.

A day in this job

The day starts early, often before light, with feeding and checking the animals. You might spend the morning moving sheep between fields, dosing cattle, or hitching up a trailer for a feed run. Afternoons swing with the season: fencing, muck spreading, drilling a field, or bedding down the barns. You finish tired, smelling of the farm, with everything fed and shut in safe.

How AI is changing it

Farms are filling up with sensors, from collars that track a cow's health to software that plans feed, field work and paperwork. AI handles the records, the compliance forms and much of the route and machine planning. Knowing when a ewe is about to lamb, feeling the weather turn, and working with your hands stay human.

What you need to start

There are no set entry qualifications. You learn on the job, and agricultural college courses and apprenticeships can build on that. A driving licence often helps, since farms are rarely on a bus route.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. See what farm work is now Read farm worker profiles. The job has changed, with robotics, data and animal care side by side.
  2. Get on a farm Ask local farms about weekend, holiday or harvest work. Seasonal jobs are the traditional way in.
  3. Learn the kit safely Short courses cover tractors, livestock handling and safe use of machinery. Lantra is the main training body for land-based skills.
  4. Add a qualification Agriculture courses at levels 1 to 3 run at agricultural colleges, and apprenticeships let you earn while learning.
  5. Apply for year-round roles With a season and some training behind you, apply for permanent stock or general farm work.
  6. Grow into the skilled jobs Herdsperson, machinery operator or farm supervisor roles follow with experience, and some workers move into farm management.
