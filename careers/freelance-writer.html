Freelance writer

Freelance writers earn by saying things clearly for people who can't. Every business has a website, a newsletter and a pitch that need words, and most owners dread writing them. People who love reading, notice how sentences work and can meet a deadline thrive here. Be honest with yourself about the hard parts: income arrives in waves, rejection is routine, and AI now drafts passable copy in seconds. That is exactly why voice matters more, not less. Clients are drowning in average words. A writer with taste, a point of view and reliability stands out faster than ever.

A day in this job

Mornings often go to the hardest writing, before email wakes up. You might draft a client's web page, edit yesterday's newsletter and send two pitches to new prospects. Afternoons bring calls, invoices and research for the next piece. Some days are all writing; others are all chasing the next job.

How AI is changing it

AI already drafts articles, posts and outlines in seconds, and many clients use it themselves. That removes the routine word-count work and raises the bar for everything else. What stays human is the idea, the voice, the judgement about what is true and worth saying, and the trust a client places in you.

What you need to start

There is no single entry route; people arrive with all kinds of qualifications or simply relevant experience. In this branch a strong portfolio of real work counts for more than certificates.

Typical pay: £720 a week (about £37,400 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Find your voice Read widely and write often until your own way of putting things shows up.
  2. Write in public Start a newsletter, blog or LinkedIn series. A regular slot builds voice and proof.
  3. Make AI your studio Use AI to research, outline and redraft. Keep the ideas and the final word yours.
  4. Build a three-piece portfolio Three strong, varied pieces beat thirty average ones.
  5. Pitch for work Approach small firms that clearly need help saying what they do. There are thousands.
  6. Turn work into packages Turn one-off jobs into repeatable packages clients can buy.
