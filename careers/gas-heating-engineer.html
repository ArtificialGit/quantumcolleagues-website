Gas / heating engineer

Gas and heating engineers keep homes warm and safe, servicing boilers, fixing breakdowns and installing new systems. The work is skilled, regulated and respected, and it pays well because the responsibility is real. It suits careful, methodical people who like being trusted with work that matters. The honest bits: winter is your busiest season, breakdown call-outs can be unsociable, and the paperwork is strict for good reason. With heating moving towards heat pumps, engineers who hold gas skills and add low-carbon ones will be wanted on both sides of the transition.

A day in this job

Mornings are usually booked services: checking flues, testing pressures and certifying appliances. Afternoons often bring a breakdown, a cold house and a relieved customer when the heating fires back up. Every job ends with the safety checks and the record keeping that the registration card in your pocket demands.

How AI is changing it

Scheduling, service reminders, quotes and compliance records are all getting quicker, with AI handling the drafting. Fault databases can suggest likely causes before you open the case. The gas work itself, the safety judgement and the trust that lets you into thousands of homes stay entirely human.

What you need to start

There are no formal academic entry requirements, and most engineers train through an apprenticeship or college course with on-the-job training. To work on gas legally you must be registered with Gas Safe.

Typical pay: £670 a week (about £34,800 a year).
Official UK pay for "Plumbers and heating and ventilating installers and repairers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.
