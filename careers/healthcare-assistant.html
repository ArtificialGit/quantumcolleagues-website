Healthcare assistant

Healthcare assistants are the hands and eyes of a ward or clinic: washing, feeding, taking observations, and keeping patients comfortable and safe. It suits calm, practical people who can be kind under pressure. Be honest with yourself: shifts include nights, the work is physical, and you will see hard things. In return you get a real ladder, healthcare assistant to nursing associate to registered nurse, with funded routes at every rung, and the certainty that the NHS and care sector genuinely need you.

A day in this job

A ward shift starts with handover, then washes, breakfasts and observation rounds: blood pressure, temperature, pulse. You chat while you work, because reassurance is half the job. There are beds to change, samples to run and results to log. By handover you are tired, but you know exactly what you did for people.

How AI is changing it

Observations increasingly log themselves, notes can be dictated and summarised, and rotas and supplies are sorted by software. AI helps flag which patient needs attention next. The bedside itself, the washing, the noticing, the reassuring word, stays completely human.

What you need to start

There are no formal academic entry requirements, and training is given on the job. Care qualifications at Levels 2 and 3 are available once you start, and a DBS check is standard for health roles.

Typical pay: £480 a week (about £25,000 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Explore the ladder Read about healthcare assistant and nursing associate jobs to see the steps.
  2. Brush up English and maths Health jobs ask for basic English and maths. Free courses get you there.
  3. Start as a healthcare assistant Most healthcare assistant jobs need no qualifications to start. Attitude and care matter most.
  4. Take a nursing associate apprenticeship Earn while you train. This is the recognised path toward registered nursing.
  5. Keep climbing with funded study The Lifelong Learning Entitlement helps pay for later steps toward nursing or specialist jobs.
  6. Specialise where you're needed Mental health, learning disability, theatre or community nursing all want people.
