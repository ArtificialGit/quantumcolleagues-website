Heat-pump installer

Heat-pump installers put in the low-carbon heating systems replacing gas boilers across the UK. It suits practical people who like a mix of plumbing, electrics and problem solving, and who want to be early in a growing trade rather than late in a shrinking one. The honest bits: heavy units to shift, outdoor work in winter, and customers who need patient explanations. Demand is real and rising as homes move away from fossil fuels, and installers who train now will be in short supply for years.

A day in this job

A typical day is one install or two surveys. You might spend the morning siting an outdoor unit, running pipework and wiring it in, then the afternoon commissioning the system and walking the homeowner through the controls. You finish by photographing the job and signing off the paperwork.

How AI is changing it

AI helps with the sizing calculations, the quote, the grant paperwork and the schedule, which used to eat whole evenings. Design software is getting better at suggesting the right system for a house. The install itself, the judgement on site and the homeowner's trust in you stay human.

What you need to start

There are no formal entry requirements, though GCSEs help. Most people come in through an apprenticeship or a college course with on-the-job training, or by building up experience with short courses.

Typical pay: £670 a week (about £34,800 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. See the opportunity Read heat-pump engineer profiles to understand the work, the pay and why demand keeps climbing.
  2. Start from a base trade Electrics or plumbing gives the strongest foundation, and both convert well into heat-pump work later.
  3. Check the funded routes Apprenticeships are funded at any age, and adults can often access free green-skills training too.
  4. Take a heat-pump course Short heat-pump installation courses certify you for the specialist work, and funded places are common.
  5. Certify for grant work Recognised certification lets your installs qualify for government grant schemes, which is where much of the work is.
  6. Grow once you're in Add servicing contracts and system design skills, then mentor the next trainee your firm takes on.
