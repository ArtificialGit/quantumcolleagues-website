Home-care (domiciliary) worker

Home care means visiting people in their own homes to help with getting up, meals, medication and company. Rounds run on a rota, so hours can flex around family life, and demand exists almost everywhere in the UK. It suits people who like working alone, driving between visits, and being trusted in someone's private space. Honestly, the rounds can feel rushed, and pay arrangements for travel time vary, so choose your employer with care. For the right person, being the friendly face someone waits for all day is worth a lot.

A day in this job

You start early, checking your round on your phone, then drive to the first visit. Each call is different: helping someone out of bed, making breakfast, prompting tablets, writing a quick note before you leave. You fit lunch in between visits. Evenings can bring a second shift for bedtime calls.

How AI is changing it

Apps now plan the round, log each visit and draft the notes, so less of your day goes on paperwork in the car. Alerts flag when something looks off between visits. Walking through the door, noticing a change, and being trusted stay human.

What you need to start

There are no formal academic entry requirements, and training is given on the job. You usually need to be 18 or over, a DBS check is likely, and most rounds need a driving licence.

Typical pay: £440 a week (about £22,900 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Read the round Read home care worker profiles to see what a visiting round involves, from personal care to medication prompts and notes.
  2. Sort your transport Most rounds need a driver with their own car, so check licence, insurance and mileage arrangements before you apply anywhere.
  3. Try care first Volunteer with a lunch club, befriending scheme or care service to test whether one to one care work genuinely fits you.
  4. Start the Care Certificate Employers train you towards this standard on the job, covering safeguarding, dignity, moving safely and record keeping.
  5. Apply to local providers Domiciliary teams recruit constantly, so apply to two or three, ask about travel pay, and compare rotas honestly.
  6. Grow once you're in Funded study can carry you towards senior carer, medication lead or nursing routes while you keep earning.
