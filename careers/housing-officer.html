Housing officer

Housing officers manage homes and tenancies for councils and housing associations, from lettings and repairs to arrears and neighbour disputes. It suits calm, fair people who can hold a boundary kindly and keep good records. Be honest about it: caseloads are real, some conversations are hard, and you will meet people at their lowest. It is worth considering now because housing need is not going away, the sector recruits steadily nationwide, and the entry routes are unusually open.

A day in this job

Mornings often start with tenancy visits or a new-tenant sign-up. You come back to messages about repairs, arrears and neighbour complaints, and work through them. Afternoons might bring a case conference with support services or a difficult phone call handled with care. Notes and follow-ups close the day.

How AI is changing it

AI now writes up case notes, letters and reports, and keeps track of repairs and arrears. The doorstep does not automate. What stays human is what tenants remember: being listened to, treated fairly and backed by someone who knows the estate and its people.

What you need to start

You can get in with a range of qualifications or relevant experience. Housing qualifications run from Level 2 to Level 5, and some posts ask for professional ones.

Typical pay: £580 a week (about £30,200 a year).
