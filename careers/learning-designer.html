Learning designer

Learning designers build the courses, workshops and materials that help people actually learn, at work, online and in communities. With so many people retraining, someone has to design training worth the time, and plenty of what exists is not. It suits people who explain things well, care whether the learner got it, and enjoy structure as much as creativity. The hard parts are real: training budgets get cut when times tighten, and you will sometimes fight for learners against tick-box compliance. Demand is growing though, the field welcomes career changers, and your first samples can come from things you already know.

A day in this job

The morning goes to storyboarding a course on a new system, turning an expert's brain dump into steps a beginner can follow. You interview that expert before lunch, asking the obvious questions learners would ask. The afternoon is building: writing scenarios, drafting quiz questions and testing the flow. You end by reviewing feedback from last week's pilot.

How AI is changing it

AI drafts course content, quiz banks and scenarios in minutes, and summarises expert interviews for you, so production is no longer the bottleneck. What stays human is the design: knowing what real learners need, spotting what is confusing or unfair, and caring whether anyone actually changed on Monday morning.

What you need to start

No formal qualifications are required to start; most people qualified in another field first, then added a teaching or training qualification, which run at Levels 3 to 5. A couple of real course samples will carry you a long way.

Typical pay: £670 a week (about £34,800 a year).
Official UK pay for "Other vocational and industrial trainers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Explore learning design Look at learning designer and training team jobs to see what the work involves.
  2. Design one short course Take something you know and turn it into a 30 minute lesson. That's your first sample.
  3. Learn the method A short course in learning design or e-learning adds structure to your instinct.
  4. Use AI to build faster AI drafts content and assessments so you focus on the learning design.
  5. Build a small portfolio and apply Two or three samples are enough. Training teams value real world experience.
  6. Teach people to use AI Courses on using AI well are in huge demand. You can lead that.
