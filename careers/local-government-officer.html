Local-government officer

Local-government officers make council services actually happen: housing, parks, benefits, licensing, school admissions and much more. It suits steady, fair-minded people who like serving their own area and want secure, pension-backed work. The honest parts: pay starts modest, some processes are slow, and residents are sometimes angry with the system you represent. It is a good moment to join because councils recruit constantly, progression from inside is real, and officers who use new tools well are valued.

A day in this job

You might open the day working through resident queries and case updates. Mid-morning could bring a team meeting or a site visit. Afternoons go on letters, reports and updating records, with the odd call from a councillor. You close cases where you can and hand over the rest cleanly.

How AI is changing it

AI is taking on the letters, case summaries, form-filling and routine queries that used to fill an officer's day. The decisions do not go with them. What stays human is judgement and fairness: weighing a case, hearing a resident properly and knowing how your patch really works.

What you need to start

Most people get in with GCSEs, and some posts ask for keyboard skills. Training is given on the job, with administration qualifications available at Levels 2 and 3.

Typical pay: £540 a week (about £28,100 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Explore council careers Read about council jobs across services, from housing to parks and libraries.
  2. Build the core skills Clear writing, basic data and digital admin are used every day.
  3. Watch your council's jobs board Councils advertise jobs all the time. Business support jobs are the common way in.
  4. Consider a council apprenticeship Many councils run funded apprenticeships at all ages.
  5. Use AI to serve residents better Staff who can use AI for casework get through it faster and stand out.
  6. Move up from inside Councils promote from within. Get in first, then specialise.
