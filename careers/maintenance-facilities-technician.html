Maintenance / facilities technician

Maintenance and facilities technicians keep buildings, plant and machinery running: heating, ventilation, electrics, pumps and the hundred small systems nobody notices until they stop. It suits steady, curious people who like variety and being the one who can fix it. The honest bits: some roles carry on-call shifts, breakdowns choose their own timing, and you often work around a live, busy building. Every factory, hospital, school and office needs this work, and as buildings fill with smart, data-rich kit, technicians who can read it are increasingly valuable.

A day in this job

The day runs off a maintenance schedule and a fault list that rewrites itself by lunchtime. You might service an air-handling unit in the morning, trace an electrical fault after lunch, and sign off contractor work in between. Some faults take five minutes, and the interesting ones take the afternoon.

How AI is changing it

Buildings now stream sensor data, and AI flags problems before they become breakdowns, shifting the job from firefighting towards prevention. Work orders, parts and compliance logs are quicker to manage too. The physical fix, the safe isolation and the judgement on the spot stay human.

What you need to start

Qualifications are not always required, though GCSEs can help. Training typically runs through an apprenticeship, and good eyesight and normal colour vision are needed.

Typical pay: £690 a week (about £35,900 a year).
