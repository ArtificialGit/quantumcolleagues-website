Manufacturing systems engineer

Manufacturing systems engineers design the production lines that make things faster, safer and less wasteful. It suits people who like seeing a whole system, enjoy improving how things are done, and are happy to lead teams and budgets. Be honest about the hard parts: changes to a live line carry real pressure, and some employers run weekend rotas. The reason to consider it now is that UK makers are investing in automation and lower waste, and they need engineers who can redesign how the work flows.

A day in this job

A day might begin with plans for a new piece of equipment or a change to a line. You could build a model in CAD and CAM software, then test the system and study the data to find a better method. Later you manage the project, keep the budget on track and report progress to senior managers.

How AI is changing it

AI is changing how factories are planned and run. Software can simulate a production line, predict bottlenecks and suggest improvements before anything moves on the shop floor. Engineers then spend more time choosing between options and leading the change. Responsibility for a safe, workable line stays with people.

What you need to start

Most people come in with a foundation degree, a higher national diploma or a degree in a related subject. Level 5 higher and Level 6 degree apprenticeships let you earn while you study. A master's degree is an option if you already hold another engineering qualification. Incorporated or Chartered Engineer status can follow with experience.

Typical pay: £870 a week (about £45,200 a year).
