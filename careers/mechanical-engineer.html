Mechanical engineer

Mechanical engineers design, test and improve the machines and systems that others build and run. It suits people who are strong at maths and physics, who like turning ideas into plans, and who want to lead projects over time. Be honest about the hard parts: the climb to chartered status takes years, and site visits can mean all weathers. The reason to consider it now is the push for cleaner energy and transport, which needs engineers who can make machines more efficient and use lower carbon materials.

A day in this job

A day might begin with a design on CAD software, turning a research idea into a technical plan. You could then run tests on a system and study the data to see what to improve. In the afternoon you check that machinery is safe, review maintenance at a plant, and brief the technicians and designers on your project team.

How AI is changing it

AI tools are speeding up design and analysis. Software can test many design options and spot patterns in test data much faster than before. That frees engineers to spend more time on judgement: which design is safe, which is worth building and how it fits the people who will use it. Signing off that judgement stays with you.

What you need to start

Most mechanical engineers take a degree or a higher national diploma, which usually needs two or three A levels including maths and physics. A degree apprenticeship lets you earn while you study. You can also start as a mechanical engineering technician and train up on the job. Chartered Engineer status comes later, through the Institution of Mechanical Engineers.

Typical pay: £940 a week (about £48,900 a year).
