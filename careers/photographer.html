Photographer

Photographers freeze the moments people pay to keep: weddings, products, food, portraits and houses for sale. Anyone can take a photo now, which is precisely the point; clients pay for the person who reliably gets the shot when it matters. It suits patient observers who handle people well and stay calm when the schedule slips. Be clear about the hard parts: the market is crowded, AI images now fill much of stock and advertising, and income swings with the seasons. The work that survives is the work that has to be real: real people, real events, real trust.

A day in this job

The morning brings a family shoot, working fast while a toddler still cooperates. Back home you cut hundreds of frames down to the thirty that sing, then edit colour and light. You answer two enquiries, post one image with the client's blessing and prepare kit for tomorrow. Deliveries go out before you stop.

How AI is changing it

AI edits, retouches, tags and sorts images in a fraction of the old time, and generated pictures are common in advertising. What stays human is the real moment: being trusted in the room, directing people kindly, and the eye that knows which frame is the one.

What you need to start

No set qualifications are required, though most entrants have GCSEs or A levels and some hands-on experience. Vocational photography qualifications run at Levels 1 to 3, and a strong portfolio opens more doors than any of them.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).
Official UK pay for "Photographers, audio-visual and broadcasting equipment operators" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Master your kit A phone or entry camera plus free editing software is enough. Learn light before you spend on gear.
  2. Pick a niche Think weddings, food, trades, pets or families. Specialists get booked and remembered; generalists get scrolled past.
  3. Shoot for real people Photograph local events, causes and small businesses for free at first. A tight set of strong images opens doors.
  4. Consider a funded qualification Photography qualifications run at Levels 1 to 3 if you want structure, and funding is sometimes available for adults.
  5. Charge and deliver Set simple prices and start with two or three local clients. Reliable delivery starts the referrals.
  6. Grow once you're in Raise prices as your diary fills. Teaching, prints and licensing add income between bookings.
