Podcast / audio producer

Podcast and audio producers shape raw conversation into something worth an hour of a stranger's time. Businesses, charities and independent hosts all want shows, and most have no idea how much work sits between recording and publishing. It suits good listeners with an ear for pace, people who enjoy structure and quiet craft. Honestly, budgets are often small, many shows fade after ten episodes, and freelance work arrives unevenly. The field is young though, good producers are genuinely scarce, and one well-made series can become the calling card that brings the next three clients.

A day in this job

You spend the morning editing this week's episode: cutting rambles, balancing voices and laying in music. Midday you join a recording session as the calm voice guiding a nervous host. The afternoon goes to show notes, clips for social media and planning next week's guest. You publish, then check how listeners responded.

How AI is changing it

AI transcribes instantly, removes filler words, cleans audio and drafts show notes and clips, so the tedious half of production is disappearing. What stays human is the ear: shaping a story, knowing what to cut, coaching a host, and earning the trust that keeps a show alive.

What you need to start

There are no set academic requirements; most people bring GCSEs or A levels and evidence of hands-on experience. Finished work you can play to people counts for more than certificates.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Learn the basics Free courses cover recording, editing and how stories work in sound. Your ear matters more than your microphone.
  2. Produce a real show Make a short series for yourself or a local cause. Finished episodes are the proof clients want.
  3. Use AI in the edit AI transcribes, tidies audio and drafts notes and clips, so you can focus on the story.
  4. Get funded support If you're 16 to 30, the King's Trust offers free help turning production into a small business.
  5. Find clients Businesses, charities and independent hosts all pay for production. Pitch where you already listen and offer an improvement.
  6. Grow into producing Move from editing to shaping whole shows. The producer who owns the story earns the trust and the fee.
