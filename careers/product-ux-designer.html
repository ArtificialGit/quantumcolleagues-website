Product / UX designer

Product and UX designers decide how apps and services actually work: what you tap, where it takes you and whether you swear at it. It is design as problem solving, closer to psychology than decoration, and it pays better than most creative roles. It suits people who are curious about why humans get stuck and patient enough to test rather than guess. It has downsides: you will spend time in meetings defending simple ideas, and junior roles are competitive. Every sector now builds digital services though, and designers who show clear thinking in a portfolio get noticed.

A day in this job

You might watch three users try a new sign-up flow and note where they hesitate. Then you sketch a simpler version, build a quick prototype and share it with the engineers. Afternoons bring a design review and small fixes to screens already being built. You write up what you learned before logging off.

How AI is changing it

AI drafts wireframes, writes interface text and even runs rough usability checks, so first versions of screens are nearly free. What stays human is understanding people: choosing the right problem, reading real users' frustration, and holding the standard for what actually ships.

What you need to start

Most entrants have a degree or equivalent, but entry with other qualifications or solid relevant experience is possible. A portfolio of real case studies does the heavy lifting.

Typical pay: £830 a week (about £43,200 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Learn UX fundamentals Free courses cover user research, simple flows and testing. You need the method more than expensive software.
  2. Redesign something real Pick an app or service that annoys you and redesign it. Real problems make the best practice.
  3. Check funded routes Skills Bootcamps sometimes cover digital and design skills, free for many adults. A funded course adds structure fast.
  4. Build case studies Two or three case studies that show your thinking beat a wall of pretty screens. Explain every decision.
  5. Apply across sectors Product teams in every industry hire UX designers. Your portfolio matters more than any certificate here.
  6. Grow once you're in Move from single screens to whole journeys. Senior designers decide what gets built and why, not just how it looks.
