Quality control officer

Quality control officers check that products and processes meet the standard they should. It suits people with a careful eye, who like rules that make sense, and who are confident enough to stop a line when something is wrong. Be honest about the hard parts: shifts and weekend work are common, and you sometimes have to deliver unwelcome news to busy managers. The reason to consider it now is that every manufacturer needs quality people, and it is a clear way into engineering that can lead to quality manager roles.

A day in this job

You might start by inspecting a batch of products or testing part of a production process. Then you compare the results against the standards and the regulations, record what you found and report it. Later you could review a quality policy, check whether a recent change to a process has worked, or train others on the quality equipment and software.

How AI is changing it

AI is taking on more of the routine checking. Cameras and software can inspect parts on a line and flag defects faster than the eye. That shifts the job towards understanding why problems happen and fixing the process. Deciding what counts as good enough, and signing off the result, stays with people.

What you need to start

You can start as a quality control assistant and train on the job. A Level 4 Quality Practitioner apprenticeship usually asks for four or five GCSEs at grades 9 to 4, plus A levels or equivalent. Degrees in manufacturing, engineering or science also lead in, and knowing British quality standards helps.

Typical pay: £680 a week (about £35,400 a year).
