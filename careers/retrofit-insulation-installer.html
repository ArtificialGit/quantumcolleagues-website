Retrofit / insulation installer

Retrofit and insulation installers make older homes warmer and cheaper to run: insulating walls and lofts, improving airtightness and fitting ventilation. It suits practical people who want purposeful work with a clear result, a warmer house and a lower bill. The honest bits: lofts are dusty, crawl spaces are tight, and you wear protective kit that gets sweaty. The UK has millions of older homes to bring up to standard, so this trade has a long road ahead, and funded training makes it one of the easier doors into construction.

A day in this job

You might spend the morning in a loft laying insulation and sealing gaps, and the afternoon fitting external wall panels from a scaffold. There is measuring, cutting and a lot of methodical checking, because gaps you cannot see are gaps that leak heat. You finish dusty and visibly further on than you started.

How AI is changing it

Surveys, heat-loss calculations and grant paperwork are being sped up by AI, and thermal imaging tools make problems easier to spot. Job scheduling and compliance records take minutes rather than evenings. The careful installation, the awkward corners and the judgement about each individual house stay human.

What you need to start

There are no formal entry requirements, and training is usually given on the job. Qualifications in building and construction exist at several levels, with apprenticeships in some areas.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. See the demand Read retrofit and insulation profiles to understand why millions of older homes need this work.
  2. Take a funded course Insulation and retrofit courses are widely funded, so training is one of the cheaper doors into construction.
  3. Get site-ready A CSCS card and basic safety training let you work on managed construction sites.
  4. Check support if you're under 30 The King's Trust runs free programmes and grants that help young people into construction work.
  5. Specialise and certify Retrofit coordination and assessment qualifications open the better-paid, supervisory end of the work.
  6. Grow once you're in Experience across walls, lofts and ventilation makes you the installer firms trust with the tricky houses.
