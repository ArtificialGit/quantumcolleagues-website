SEN / learning-support assistant

SEN support means working closely with children who have additional needs, often one to one: communication aids, sensory breaks, carefully built routines, and small steps celebrated hard. It suits deeply patient people who can stay calm when a day goes sideways and who find real joy in tiny wins. Honestly, it can be physically and emotionally demanding, and progress is measured in months, not days. It is also skilled, valued and in demand, and schools invest in the people who are good at it.

A day in this job

Your day is shaped around one child or a small group: meeting them at the door, settling them in, working through tasks broken into tiny steps. You watch for triggers and step in early. There are visual timetables to run, notes for the SENCO, and moments of pure delight when something clicks.

How AI is changing it

AI now helps adapt resources, draft communication plans and keep the paperwork behind support plans up to date. Some children also benefit directly from assistive tools. Reading a child's mood, keeping them safe and celebrating their wins stay entirely yours.

What you need to start

Some schools ask for qualifications, but relevant experience alone can get you in. A DBS check is mandatory.

Typical pay: £360 a week (about £18,700 a year).
Official UK pay for "Educational support assistants" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Learn about SEN Read SEN support profiles to understand the settings, the needs you might support and what the role asks of you.
  2. Get classroom time Volunteer in a local school or SEN unit, where a term of mornings builds references and genuine confidence.
  3. Take a SEN qualification Level 3 SEN courses run at most colleges and are often free for adults through government funded places.
  4. Learn assistive tools Get comfortable with communication aids and simple classroom technology, because many children rely on them every single day.
  5. Apply to schools SEN roles are sought after, so apply widely and let your volunteering stories show the patience the work needs.
  6. Grow once you're in Experienced SEN staff move towards HLTA status, specialist roles or teaching, with funded study supporting every step.
