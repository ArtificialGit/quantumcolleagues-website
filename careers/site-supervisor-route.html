Site supervisor route

Site supervisors run the day-to-day of a building site: sequencing trades, keeping work safe, checking quality and solving the morning's surprises. It is the natural step up for experienced tradespeople who find themselves organising others anyway. It suits calm, respected people who can read drawings and read moods. The honest bits: the phone never stops, you carry responsibility for other people's safety, and paperwork is a large slice of the day. Construction is short of people who can lead sites well, so those who make the step are valued and paid accordingly.

A day in this job

The day starts before the trades arrive, walking the site with a coffee and a snag list. Then it is inductions, deliveries, progress checks and a dozen quick decisions that keep everyone working. You end the day updating the programme and planning tomorrow so nobody stands idle.

How AI is changing it

AI now drafts programmes, tracks materials, summarises site reports and fills the endless forms faster than any office afternoon could. Photo tools log progress automatically. What stays yours is the walk of the site, the call on safety, and the respect of the crew who follow your lead.

What you need to start

There are no formal entry requirements, though GCSEs help. Most supervisors come up through a trade, learning via an apprenticeship or college course plus on-the-job experience and short courses.

Typical pay: £750 a week (about £39,000 a year).
Official UK pay for "Construction and building trades supervisors" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Build trade experience A few years on the tools earns the knowledge and the respect a supervisor needs.
  2. Explore the step up Read supervisor and site manager profiles to see what the role adds and what it pays.
  3. Add site management tickets Site safety and first aid qualifications are the recognised tickets that open supervisor roles.
  4. Use funded learning Free courses for adults and employer funding can cover much of the management training.
  5. Learn planning with AI Scheduling, materials and progress reports are faster with AI, and supervisors who use it stand out.
  6. Step up and keep growing Move into assistant site manager roles, then site manager, as your record of safe delivery builds.
