Supported-housing worker

Supported-housing workers help people with extra needs, such as those leaving homelessness, care or crisis, to keep a tenancy and live well. It suits patient, steady people who can build trust with someone the system has often failed. The honest parts: shifts can include evenings, the stories are heavy sometimes, and pay is modest for the responsibility. It is worth considering now because demand is constant, entry is open, and few jobs offer this much visible, human impact.

A day in this job

You start with a handover from the last shift and a plan for the day. Mornings might mean helping a tenant with a benefits form or a doctor's appointment. Afternoons bring key-work sessions, a house meeting or welcoming a new resident. You write up notes and hand over before you leave.

How AI is changing it

AI now drafts support plans, case notes and referral letters, which frees real time with residents. The relationship is the job, and it stays yours: sitting with someone in a bad week, celebrating a good one and being the steady person they trust.

What you need to start

You can get in with a range of qualifications or relevant experience, and housing qualifications are available from Level 2 to Level 5. A DBS check is usually needed because you work with vulnerable people.

Typical pay: £580 a week (about £30,200 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Understand the role Read supported-housing profiles to see the settings, from hostels to move-on flats, and what workers actually do.
  2. Volunteer first Homelessness and housing charities teach fast. A regular weekly shift builds skills and a solid reference.
  3. Add safeguarding basics Short safeguarding and mental-health awareness courses strengthen your application and prepare you for the work.
  4. Take a funded qualification Housing qualifications run from Level 2 to Level 5, and funded routes exist at every age.
  5. Apply Housing associations and support providers recruit steadily nationwide. Values-based interviews reward honesty over polish.
  6. Grow once you're in Specialise in complex needs, move into tenancy support or step up to senior worker and team leader.
