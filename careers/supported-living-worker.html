Supported-living worker

Supported living means helping adults with learning disabilities, autism or mental health needs live their own lives: cooking together, managing money, getting to appointments, building routines. It suits people who are steady, respectful and happy to encourage rather than take over. The honest part: progress can be slow, some behaviour is challenging, and shifts include evenings and weekends. The reward is watching someone do a thing for themselves that nobody thought they could. Providers recruit steadily across the UK and will train you from scratch.

A day in this job

Your shift might start in someone's flat, planning the day over a cup of tea. You support shopping trips, cooking, medication and appointments, always encouraging independence rather than doing it for them. Notes go into an app as you go. Some shifts are lively, others are quiet company.

How AI is changing it

Support plans, daily logs and rota juggling are increasingly handled by software, with AI drafting the routine records. That frees your attention for the person in front of you. Patience, encouragement and the judgement about when to step in stay yours.

What you need to start

There are no formal academic entry requirements, and employers train you towards a care qualification on the job. You usually need to be 18 or over, and a DBS check is likely.

Typical pay: £440 a week (about £22,900 a year).
Official UK pay for "Care workers and home carers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Understand the work Read supported living profiles to learn the settings, the people you would support and what building independence really means.
  2. Volunteer or shadow A few sessions with a local service show you the pace, the challenges and the small wins that make it worthwhile.
  3. Learn the basics Start the Care Certificate and safeguarding training, which most employers provide on the job from your first week.
  4. Check funded routes Care qualifications at Levels 2 and 3 are often funded for adults, so ask employers and colleges what they cover.
  5. Apply to providers Supported living providers recruit steadily nationwide, so apply to a few and ask about training, shifts and new starter support.
  6. Grow once you're in Experienced workers move into senior support, positive behaviour roles or team leading, with funded study helping each step.
