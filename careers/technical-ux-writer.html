Technical / UX writer

Technical and UX writers make complicated things understandable: help pages, app text, manuals and the words on every button. It is the quiet, well-paid end of writing, and demand comes from software firms, banks, government and anywhere products confuse people. It suits patient writers who enjoy precision and asking basic questions until things make sense. Honestly, it is less glamorous than journalism, and you will spend time with engineers and messy products. The work is steadier than most freelance writing though, clarity is scarce, and a few strong before-and-after samples can get you interviews without a specialist degree.

A day in this job

You might spend the morning interviewing an engineer about a new feature, then turn their answers into a help page a stressed customer can follow. After lunch you test the words in the app itself, trim a confusing error message and update the documentation. You finish by checking feedback on what you wrote.

How AI is changing it

AI drafts documentation, rewrites text at different reading levels and keeps style consistent across hundreds of pages. It makes one writer as productive as a small team. What stays human is deciding what users actually need, testing whether the words work, and taking responsibility for accuracy.

What you need to start

Entry is open to people with a variety of qualifications or relevant experience, and some roles ask for professional or postgraduate qualifications. Clear samples of your writing matter more than certificates here.

Typical pay: £720 a week (about £37,400 a year).

The steps, in order

  1. Understand the craft Read real help pages and app text. Technical writers make complicated things feel simple, and that skill is scarce.
  2. Take a short course Free short courses cover plain English, documentation and writing for screens. Structure turns instinct into method.
  3. Build before-and-after samples Rewrite a confusing manual, help page or app flow. Showing the before and after proves your value instantly.
  4. Look at funded qualifications If you want a formal qualification later, funded routes can help cover the cost. Samples still matter most.
  5. Apply to product teams Software firms, banks and government all hire writers who explain clearly. Your portfolio of samples does the talking.
  6. Grow once you're in Learn the product deeply and sit close to the engineers. Senior writers shape how whole products speak.
