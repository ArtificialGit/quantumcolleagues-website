Therapy / allied-health assistant

Therapy assistants support physiotherapists, occupational therapists and speech and language therapists: setting up sessions, practising exercises with patients, and cheering on slow, hard won progress. It suits encouragers, people who can keep someone going through a frustrating recovery. It is honest to say you follow plans that others write, and pay starts modest. But trusts hire assistants without prior qualifications, the work is varied and warm, and apprenticeship routes can carry you all the way to registered therapist.

A day in this job

Mornings might be helping a stroke patient practise walking, then setting up equipment for a clinic. You run through exercise plans, note how each person got on, and flag anything odd to the therapist. Afternoons could be a home visit or a group session. You finish knowing who made progress today.

How AI is changing it

Session notes, exercise plans and appointment scheduling are increasingly drafted by software, and AI can suggest adjustments from progress data. The encouragement, the watchful eye on how someone really moves, and the celebrating of small wins stay human.

What you need to start

Trusts hire therapy assistants with no prior qualification, and experience from care, sport or volunteering all counts. A DBS check is standard for health roles.

The steps, in order

  1. Explore allied health Read therapy assistant profiles across physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech therapy to see which kind of recovery work draws you.
  2. Build people experience Care work, sports coaching or volunteering all count as evidence, so gather some and note what each taught you.
  3. Learn the language A short free course in anatomy or health basics helps you follow the team's language and shine at interview.
  4. Apply for assistant posts NHS trusts hire therapy assistants with no prior qualification, so apply with your people experience front and centre.
  5. Settle in and learn Your first months teach you sessions, equipment and note keeping, and therapists gladly grow assistants who ask good questions.
  6. Train up over time Funded apprenticeship routes run from assistant towards registered therapist, so ask about them once you have found your feet.
