Videographer

Videographers film and edit the stories businesses, families and communities want told: promos, weddings, events and training films. The kit barrier has collapsed; a phone and free software are a working studio, so the trade now runs on eye, timing and reliability. It suits people who like being out in the world, put strangers at ease and enjoy the edit as much as the shoot. Be honest about the costs: weekends get eaten, editing hours are long, and freelance income comes in waves. Small businesses everywhere want affordable video though, and referrals compound quickly for people who deliver.

A day in this job

The morning brings a shoot at a local business, capturing interviews and detail shots before the light changes. The afternoon disappears into the edit: choosing takes, colour, sound and captions. In between you answer enquiries, send an invoice and back everything up twice. Evenings sometimes go to filming events, because that is when life happens.

How AI is changing it

AI now cuts rough edits, transcribes interviews, generates subtitles and steadies shaky footage, so the mechanical hours are shrinking fast. What stays human is being there: framing the moment as it happens, drawing a real story out of nervous people, and making the final cut feel true.

What you need to start

There are no set academic requirements, though most people have GCSEs or A levels plus some hands-on experience behind a camera. Vocational qualifications exist at Levels 1 to 3, but a showreel matters more.

Typical pay: £610 a week (about £31,700 a year).
Official UK pay for "Photographers, audio-visual and broadcasting equipment operators" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Master the kit you have A phone plus free editing tools is a working studio now.
  2. Publish weekly Post regularly about local stories and small businesses. Skill and audience grow together.
  3. Choose your focus Focus on weddings, trades, food or charities. Specialists get hired. Generalists get scrolled past.
  4. Build a reel and rate card A short showreel plus clear prices makes booking you easy.
  5. Charge for your work Start with two or three paying local clients. Let the referrals build from there.
  6. Scale with AI editing AI speeds the edit so you can take on more without losing quality.
