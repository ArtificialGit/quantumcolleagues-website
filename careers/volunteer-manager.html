Volunteer manager

Volunteer managers recruit, train and look after the people who give their time for free, which is most of the charity workforce. It suits warm organisers who can welcome a nervous newcomer and still keep a rota straight. Honestly, it can be tiring: volunteers come and go, budgets are tight, and pay is modest across the sector. It is worth a look now because almost every medium-sized charity needs one, and leading volunteers is the classic proof you can lead people.

A day in this job

The day often opens with messages from volunteers about shifts and swaps. You might run an induction for new starters or visit a group mid-morning. Afternoons are for planning rotas, writing a thank-you note or two and sorting training. You end by checking tomorrow's sessions are covered.

How AI is changing it

AI now handles rotas, reminder messages, records and much of the recruitment admin. That gives you back time with actual people. The core of the role stays human: making someone feel welcome, noticing when a volunteer is struggling, and building the trust that keeps people coming back.

What you need to start

Employers usually ask for a youth and community work qualification at Level 2 or 3, and apprenticeships are available. Expect a DBS check, and some roles ask you to be over 21.

Typical pay: £550 a week (about £28,600 a year).
Official UK pay for "Youth and community workers" (ONS ASHE 2022, via LMI for All). A guide, not a promise.

The steps, in order

  1. Understand the role Read volunteer-management profiles to see what recruiting, training and supporting volunteers involves day to day.
  2. Lead a volunteer team Offer to coordinate volunteers for a cause you care about. Even a small rota counts as leadership.
  3. Add safeguarding basics A short safeguarding course is expected across the sector and reassures every employer you meet.
  4. Consider a funded qualification Apprenticeships and youth and community qualifications at Level 2 or 3 are the recognised routes into paid roles.
  5. Apply for volunteer-manager roles Most medium-sized charities employ one. Lead with the team you led and what they achieved together.
  6. Grow once you're in Build towards managing services or whole programmes. Volunteer leadership is respected right across the sector.
