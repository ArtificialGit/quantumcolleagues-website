Thoughts / Bracton

Change the model. Keep the rules.

AI Thoughts for SMEs · 18 November 2026

A better AI model arrives every few months. That’s good news, but it raises an awkward question for a regulated organisation. If your safeguards live inside the model, what happens to them when the model changes?

Ours don’t live there. Admin Core runs inside Bracton, our deterministic safety layer around AI models. Bracton is ordinary code, not another model, and it meets the test we set for every safeguard: it holds without the AI’s cooperation. A rule that a model merely agrees to follow is an aspiration, not a control.

Bracton puts seven protections round the model.

  • Guarded inputs. Nothing reaches the model until our code has cleaned it, checked who sent it and decided what kind of work it is.
  • No direct power. The model drafts and proposes. It holds no send button and has no say over who hears from it, and live actions stay switched off unless the system is cleared to act.
  • Checked outputs. Everything it drafts is checked before it goes out. The policies it cites and the figures it quotes must match your own sources, and anything it can’t prove goes to a person.
  • Written limits. What each colleague may do is written down outside the model and enforced in code, not left to its judgement.
  • People decide. Anything with consequence stops with a named person, and the approval is recorded against them.
  • A full record. Every message reaches a recorded end, and every decision shows what was checked and why.
  • Safe by default. Each colleague is kept apart, runs on local models and is watched for faults, and when it’s in doubt it stops.

We named it after Henry de Bracton, an English judge of the 1200s who is remembered for the line that even the King is under the law. Nothing sits above the rules in Bracton either, and that includes the model.

Bracton is the third part of how we work. Gated Autonomy is how we build and change our AI. Our threshold model sets the line for each colleague, so everyone knows what it may do on its own and what waits for a person. Bracton holds that line while the colleague works.

Because every protection sits outside the model, any model can do the drafting and the protections stay the same. We can move routine work to a smaller, cheaper model, or bring in a stronger one where judgement matters, and the checks, the approvals and the audit trail stay as they were. A new model still passes through Gated Autonomy before it goes live, but nobody has to rebuild your safeguards each time.

On 18 November the Commons Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee published Guardrails for growth, its report on AI safety and security. Its argument is one we share. AI will only spread through the economy if people trust it. Bracton complements the report from the other end, where AI meets your inbox, your records and the people you serve.

Your safeguards shouldn’t belong to whoever supplies the model. Models will keep changing, and your rules shouldn’t have to.
