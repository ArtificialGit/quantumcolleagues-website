Guardrails for growth start where AI does the work.

AI Thoughts for SMEs · 18 November 2026

On 18 November the Commons Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee published Guardrails for growth, its report on AI safety and security. Its argument is one we share. AI will only spread through the economy if people trust it, and recent incidents have damaged that trust, including AI agents that bypassed their safeguards.

The report looks at the firms that build the most capable models and at the law around them. It finds ten gaps, from missing risk thresholds to the lack of mandatory incident reporting, and it asks the Government to set out how new and existing law will close them.

That work matters, but organisations that use AI can’t wait for a new law. A care home, a school office or an accountancy practice already puts AI to work on its own inbox and its own records, and its safeguards have to hold today, whichever model sits underneath.

That’s where Bracton comes in. The committee doesn’t want public safety to rest on the voluntary cooperation of the AI firms, and we don’t want your safety to rest on the cooperation of an AI model. Bracton is our deterministic safety layer around AI models, and Admin Core runs inside it. Its seven protections sit outside the model, so none of them relies on the model behaving.

Several of the committee’s gaps have a counterpart in your own office. It wants clear risk thresholds, and our threshold model writes down what each colleague may do on its own and what waits for a person. It wants serious incidents reported, and Bracton gives every message a recorded end and shows what was checked. It wants clearer liability and redress, and at your end Bracton stops anything with consequence with a named person, whose approval is recorded against them.

None of this replaces what the committee asks of the Government and the AI firms. Bracton complements it from the other end, where AI meets your inbox, your records and the people you serve.

Because every protection sits outside the model, your safeguards stay put when the model changes. You’ll find the seven protections in our Thought on Bracton, and the committee’s report on the UK Parliament website.
