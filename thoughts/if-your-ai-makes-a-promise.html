Thoughts / AI promises

If your AI makes a promise, you’ve made it.

Social care · 24 November 2026

A tenant in Raleigh, North Carolina, told her landlord’s leasing office that she’d pay her overdue rent at the end of the month, and an email came back to say that would work. Eight days later she was served with an eviction notice.

She told WRAL News that the office then explained the email - its AI assistant had sent it, and it had got it wrong. WRAL reports that the email didn’t say an AI had written it, and that the landlord hadn’t replied to its questions when the story ran.

I don’t know the whole story, so I won’t judge it. I do know the question it leaves behind - who answers for a promise that software makes in your name?

My answer is that you do. If your AI makes a promise, you’ve made it, and “the chatbot did it” won’t mean much to the person who relied on it.

Now put that email in a UK care home. A daughter asks for more time on her mum’s fees, and something says yes at 11pm. The provider answers to that family for every word sent in its name, and I’d expect its regulator to see it the same way.

None of that is a reason to keep AI away from care admin. AI is support and extra capacity for a stretched team, and it can read, sort, draft and chase all day. The answer is in how you set it up, and we hold ourselves to three things.

Written limits. What the AI may do on its own is written down outside the model and enforced in code, not left to its judgement.

People decide. Anything with consequence, such as money, a contract or a complaint, stops with a named person, and the approval is recorded against them.

A full record. Every message reaches a recorded end, and every decision shows what was checked and why.

That’s how Admin Core works, inside Bracton, our safety layer around AI models.
